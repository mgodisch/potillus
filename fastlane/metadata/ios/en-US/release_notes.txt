This version brings the iPhone app in line with its Android counterpart and fixes what a full review found.

A fresh installation now creates the built-in preset drinks, so the drinks list is no longer empty at first launch, and importing a backup with "Replace" really replaces the catalogue. Statistics now begin on the day you installed the app, so the days before it are no longer counted as alcohol-free, and restoring a device backup onto a new phone no longer hides the history it restored. An error message on the Today screen can now be dismissed; its OK button used to leave the message on screen.

The app lock engages immediately on a cold start, and the screens follow the clock: the blood-alcohol estimate updates every minute, and Today, Statistics, Calendar and the drink list's capacity dots move to the new day on their own. Backup and import messages, the Edit button and the Face ID prompt appear in the selected language, and the in-app guide and license text read as proper paragraphs.

Editing and deleting entries now follow iOS conventions. On Today and Calendar you tap an entry to edit it and swipe it away to delete it, with a confirmation first; on the drink list you swipe a drink to reveal Edit and Delete. Each list's Edit button in the toolbar reveals the same delete controls. The Calendar can now log a drink onto the day you have selected.

On the Statistics screen the consumption chart now comes first, marks your daily limit and colours the days above it red, and the drink categories are drawn as a donut with their grams and shares beside it. Today, Calendar, Drinks, Statistics and Settings were reworked to match the Android layout, wording and colours. The language list offers "(System)" for following the device language, "About" moved out of Settings into the menu, and the app icon is larger and crisper.

Further review rounds fixed the drink editor for languages that write decimals with a comma, so 4,9 % can now be typed. They translated the editor's messages and the Settings footers, corrected the stated amount limit to 5,000 ml, moved the menu to the trailing More button where iOS apps keep it, matched the unlock icon to Face ID, Touch ID or passcode, and stopped an accidental swipe from discarding a half-filled form.

An exported report no longer arrives with its last sheet missing, and an export that fails explains why in your own language. Long histories keep the six most recent months in the report and sum up everything older, the weekday columns follow the report's language, and day counts use each language's plural form. A CSV export handles notes that begin with a line break or carry an accent on a quotation mark. Merging a backup leaves your own settings untouched, as it does on Android.
